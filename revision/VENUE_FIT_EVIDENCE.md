**Stated aims & scope (Elsevier):** the journal aims to advance "trustworthy, interpretable, explainable, human-centered and hybrid Artificial Intelligence as it relates to all aspects of human language," and welcomes work on "the development and application of trustworthy AI to analyze, process, or model human language across various multimodal contexts, **domains**, and intelligent systems." → Applied-domain LLM reliability evaluation is squarely in scope; there is no scope requirement to analyze internal representations.

**Why R2 may have read it as a "finance paper":** the manuscript foregrounded the credit-rating application. The fix is framing (done): lead with the elicitation-reliability / evaluation-methodology contribution and treat credit rating as the testbed.

**IMPORTANT — no exact finance+LLM precedent exists in this journal.** A full scan of all 217 works (OpenAlex, ISSN 2949-7191) plus targeted title searches ("financial", "credit", "stock", …) found NO paper that is squarely "LLM applied to a financial-decision task." The three closest:
- *Financial sentiment analysis for pre-trained language models…* (2025, 100148) — finance + language models, but BERT/FinBERT-style, not generative LLMs.
- *Sentiment analysis for stock market research: A bibliometric study* (2025, 100125) — finance, but a bibliometric review.
- *The performance of the LSTM-based code generated by LLMs in forecasting time series data* (2024, 100120) — LLMs + forecasting, not explicitly finance.

Do NOT cite any of these as a "finance + LLM" precedent — it would be an overclaim. The honest and stronger move (implemented in the R2 letter) is an intersection/novelty argument: the journal publishes finance-domain NLP AND (separately) LLM reliability/evaluation; our paper sits at their intersection and is, to our knowledge, the first LLM elicitation-reliability evaluation of a financial-decision task in the journal — filling a gap, not out of scope.
